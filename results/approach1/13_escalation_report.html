Approach 1, report 13: larger models as fixes, and as candidates for the ranker

The 2B model's own corrections (reports 10–12) reach 86.38%. This report adds fixes from outside the 2B model: the answers of Qwen3-VL-8B and 4B, and the 2B model on enlarged images. They are used two ways: on their own (route the least-confident questions to the larger model) and as extra candidates for the ranker. Qwen3-VL-2B stays the detector and the encoder of every candidate. Full test splits, 9,517 questions; every choice made on validation (6,420 questions) and test read once; rankers trained on 12,000 training questions.

Builds on report 12 (ranker v3, gating). Branch feature/gating-escalation, merged into main.

  • The 8B short answer and the 8B step-by-step answer, added as candidates, lift the ranker to +6.76 (+6.10 to +7.37) points (89.53%), against +3.60 (+3.10 to +4.10) with 2B candidates only. Every dataset gains: ChartQA +10.88, TextVQA +4.00, DocVQA +4.12, ScienceQA +8.33. Gated at 50% of questions it keeps 89.31% at 9.9 passes per question.
  • Routing alone is a strong competitor. Sending the least-confident questions to the 8B model and keeping the more confident of the two answers gives +5.52 (+4.93 to +6.10) points (3.6 passes); 30% routed gives +4.54 (+4.01 to +5.08) at 2.1 passes. With the 8B short answer as its only extra candidate the ranker matches routing; it pulls ahead only when it also has the 8B reasoning answer and the 2B candidates (about 1.2 points over the best routing).
  • A 2B → 4B → 8B cascade helps only at the smallest budget. Under 1.5 passes per question it beats routing straight to 8B by +0.79 (+0.34 to +1.25) points (8B does not fit that budget); from 2 passes up the two are equal within noise (Section 6).
  • The judge pass can be dropped. Removing the model's yes/no judgement from the ranker's inputs changes accuracy by -0.02 points and removes one of the two scoring passes per candidate: the 2B-only ranker keeps 86.38% at 5.8 passes instead of 7.0. The input that matters is source agreement: without it the ranker loses 2.49 points (Section 8).
  • The ranker transfers to a dataset it never trained on. Trained on three datasets and tested on the fourth, it keeps most of its gain on each (Section 9).
  • Measured time changes the cost picture. On one H100 an 8B short answer takes 0.16 s against 0.12 s for the 2B one, while a step-by-step answer takes 1.6–2.0 s. Counted in passes, the 8B answer is expensive and reasoning cheap; in seconds it is the other way round (Section 10).

Two of the pass-counting conventions do not match measured time. An 8B short answer counts as 4 passes but takes 1.3 times a 2B answer: at batch size 1 the GPU is far from saturated, so time grows much more slowly than parameter count. A step-by-step answer counts as 1 call but takes 14 times a short answer, because it generates up to 256–384 tokens. In seconds, an 8B answer costs about as much as one zoomed-crop answer and is the most effective single fix, while the reasoning candidates are the most expensive; the gating study with seconds as the cost is in progress (Section 13).

12. Limits

  • The larger models are of the same family as the 2B model; whether a different family helps as much is not tested.
  • Pass counts are a convention; Section 10 shows they misstate the relative cost of 8B answers and reasoning.
  • Latency is measured at batch size 1 on one GPU type; batched serving would change the ratios.
  • Four datasets, all in-domain for the ranker except in Section 9.

13. In progress
