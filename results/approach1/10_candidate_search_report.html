Approach 1, report 10: candidate search and pre-generation head training

Larger candidate pools (best-of-k up to 10, temperature 0.7 to 1.3), selectors and measurements that target the selection decision, and a sweep over how the pre-generation head is trained with its input held fixed. Qwen3-VL-2B, frozen. Full test splits: 9,517 questions (ChartQA 2,500, TextVQA 2,500, DocVQA 2,500, ScienceQA 2,017). Choices made on validation (6,420 questions); test read once per choice.

Follows report 08 (complete Approach 1 report), Section 12.6, where best-of-five on the full test split gained +0.40 points over greedy and was not distinguishable from zero.

  • The pre-generation head trains better on how often the model is right than on whether one greedy answer was right. Without a ranking term, a pass-rate target beats the greedy target on every metric; with a pairwise pass-rate ranking term every target improves and they converge. Best configuration: test AUROC 0.868 against 0.862 for the exported head, AURC 0.0448 against 0.0468. The input, the cached question embedding, is the same tensor for every configuration.

2. Why this study

Report 08 reran the correction experiments on the full test split. Best-of-five with the unified head gained +0.40 points over greedy (interval −0.04 to +0.84, p = 0.081), with an oracle of +4.8 points. Two things limited it. The candidates barely differed: all five samples agreed on 79% of questions and a question had 1.36 distinct answers on average, so the selector usually had nothing to choose. And the measurement used to pick the head, pooled correctness AUROC, compares answers to different questions, which is not the decision a selector makes. This study widens the pool, measures the selection decision directly, and asks whether the pre-generation head, which decides which questions are worth more compute, can be trained better.

3. Setup

  • Pools. For every validation and test question: the greedy answer plus 10 sampled answers at temperature 0.7, 1.0 and 1.3 (top-p 0.95, 64-token limit), sampled in one batched generate call with a seed that depends only on the question. Best-of-k for k = 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 uses the first k samples of the same pool, so the k values are nested and differ only in size. Pool k always includes the greedy answer.
  • Scores. Every distinct answer is scored by the exported mixture unified head (MLP, new loss, seed 1) and by the best linear probe of the same sweep, in joint mode, plus its answer-token probability.
  • Constant question embedding. Every head score uses the cached prompt state from the feature files the heads were trained on, never a recomputed one, so all candidates, temperatures and training targets see the same question representation. A check on a GPU node compared it with a freshly computed state: cosine 0.99993 (bfloat16 against the stored float16).
  • Train pass rates. For all 50,517 training questions, 8 samples at T = 1.0, correctness only, as targets for the pre-generation head.
  • Protocol. Temperature, k and selector are chosen on the validation pools by pooled accuracy; test is read once for that choice. The full test grid is shown for the record. Differences from greedy are paired, with a bootstrap 95% interval and an exact McNemar test.
  • Compute. 38 jobs on ICE GPU nodes (5 on A100, 13 on A40, 20 on L40S), 17.1 GPU-hours, from 2026-11-05 16:01:54 to 2026-11-05 20:06:59. No failed examples.

7. Within-question ranking: why selection gains little

The heads were trained and judged by pooled AUROC, where a question's difficulty does most of the work: an easy question's answers score high and a hard question's low, whatever the answer. Inside one question that signal is constant and only the answer can differ. Measured there, the head is a much weaker ranker.

9. Pre-generation head: a better way to train it

The pre-generation head reads only the question embedding and scores a question before any answer exists. Until now it learned whether the single greedy answer was right. That label is noisy for a question-level score: a question the model answers correctly six times in ten is labelled 1 or 0 by chance. The eight samples per training question give a pass rate, the fraction answered correctly, which is a lower-variance target and closer to what the score is used for (deciding where more samples help).

  • Input. The cached question embedding, identical for every configuration. Only the training changes.
  • Targets. greedy (greedy answer correct), pass_rate (fraction of 8 samples correct, soft BCE), blend (their mean), any_correct (at least one sample correct).
  • Loss. BCE on the target plus an optional pairwise term on the pass rate: for two questions in a batch whose pass rates differ by more than 0.1, the one with the higher rate should score higher, softplus(−(si − sj)), weight 0 or 0.5.
  • Grid. 4 targets × 3 architectures (normalized linear, MLP-256, MLP-512) × 2 learning rates × 2 weight decays × 2 dropouts × 2 ranking weights = 192 configurations, 3 seeds each, early stopping on validation AURC. Calibrated with a temperature fitted on validation greedy labels.
  • Selection. On validation, by AURC (gate quality) and separately by the routing gain; both picked the same family.

Three results. First, without the ranking term the pass-rate target beats the greedy target on AUROC, AURC and rank correlation with the pass rate; the any-correct target is the worst, since "solvable by some sample" is a different property from "usually right". Second, the pairwise pass-rate term lifts every target to about the same level, so the gain comes from the pass-rate signal however it enters the loss. Third, strong regularization (dropout 0.3) and a one-hidden-layer MLP win; the normalized linear head trails by about 0.004 AUROC. The improvement over the exported head is real but small (+0.005 AUROC, -4% AURC), and as a router for best-of-k it does not beat the exported head, because the selector it routes to gains little.

10. What it means

  • Detection was never the weak point; the correctness heads remain far better than token probability at ranking answers across questions.
  • Generation is not the weak point either once the pool is widened: a right answer is in the T = 1.3, k = 10 pool for 90.8% of test questions.
  • The weak point is choosing within a question. The heads were never trained for it, and grouped AUROC shows they are only moderately good at it. That explains every best-of-k result so far, including the small gain in report 08.
  • So the next correction step should be a selector trained on the within-question decision, using what the heads lack: agreement among samples, the answer state of each candidate in the same context, and candidates from strategies that change the evidence the model reads, not only its sampling noise.

11. Limits

  • One model size (2B), one sampling family (temperature with top-p 0.95), and greedy answers fixed by the original prompt.
  • The gate curves and the test grid are reported on test; only the main configuration was chosen on validation.
  • Pass rates use 8 samples at one temperature, so they are coarse (steps of 1/8).
  • Correctness uses the same thresholds as before (VQA score ≥ 0.5, ANLS ≥ 0.9, exact option letter), which can mark near-misses as wrong.

12. Next

A learned within-question candidate ranker, trained on the 50,517 training pools with a softmax loss over each question's candidates, using the constant question embedding, each candidate's answer state, vote share, the head and probe scores and a teacher-forced answer probability; and two correction strategies that add candidates by changing what the model reads: visual contrastive decoding against a noised image, and zoomed crops. These runs are in progress.
