The New Loss With a Corrupted Image Instead of a Swapped One

The same loss as the swap version, with h_corrupt taken from the same image after noise is mixed in. Pooled mixture, each dataset alone, sequential training, and a check of how grounding carries over between corruptions.

Model: Qwen3-VL-2B. Mixture: 50,517 train, 6,420 validation, 9,517 test rows. Every number is on the test split, mean of three seeds.

1. In brief

  • The loss is unchanged. Only the source of h_corrupt changes: the question and the model's answer stay the same, and the image is replaced by itself mixed with Gaussian noise (alpha 0.15, so 15% of the signal variance is kept) instead of by another example's image.
  • Ranking quality is not affected. Pooled AUROC with the noise version of the new loss is within 0.0024 of the old loss for every head kind, which is about the size of the seed-to-seed standard deviation (up to 0.0024).
  • Grounding rises. The share of correct answers whose score falls when the image is noised goes from 0.921 to 0.941 with the old loss to 0.971 to 1.000 with the noise version of the new loss (pre-generation, verifier, unified).
  • Grounding carries over between corruptions. Heads trained with noise score 0.907 to 0.967 on swapped images they never trained on, and heads trained with swap score 0.950 to 0.980 on noise. The old loss already scores about 0.88 to 0.95 on every corruption, so the gain is on top of a high floor.
  • Per dataset and in sequential training the picture matches the pooled one: AUROC unchanged, grounding higher. Forgetting with the noise loss is similar to the swap loss and is removed by replay in the same way.

2. What changed and why

The new loss adds a visual-grounding term to the correctness loss. For every correct answer the head scores the pair twice, once with the states of the clean image and once with the states of a corrupted image, and the term asks for the clean score to be higher:

L = L_cls + w_pre L_pre + w_pair L_pair + lambda L_ground, with L_ground = mean over correct answers of softplus(margin - (s(x_clean) - s(x_corrupt)))

The swap version builds x_corrupt from another example's image. That has a weakness: the corrupted pair is also a mismatched question and image, and a head can satisfy the term by detecting the mismatch, without looking at how well the image supports the answer. The noise version keeps the right image and removes detail, which takes away that shortcut. Noise follows the forward step of a diffusion process, x' = sqrt(a) x + sqrt(1 - a) eps with a = 0.15. Strength was chosen on 30 examples per dataset by how far the model's own answer probability falls on ScienceQA and TextVQA, so it is a reasonable setting, not a tuned optimum.

Mask (60% of a 16 by 16 grid blanked) and shuffle (4 by 4 patches permuted) were extracted for the test splits only. They are never trained on, so they work as a check that the learned grounding is not specific to one kind of corruption.

On ChartQA, TextVQA and DocVQA the old loss already lowers the score on about 0.97 or more of correct answers under noise, so there is little left for the grounding term to add and AUROC does not move. ScienceQA is where the term matters: the old loss lowers the score on only 0.69 to 0.78 of correct answers under noise, and the noise loss raises that to 0.87 to 1.0 depending on the head. ScienceQA questions can often be answered from the text alone, so a score that ignores the image is the expected outcome without the term, and ScienceQA AUROC changes by less than 0.01 either way.

7. How far to trust this

  • Order accuracy near 1.0 is partly easy. A noised image moves the hidden state a lot, so a head can learn to lower its score on noisy-looking inputs without checking whether the image supports the answer. The cross-corruption check lowers this worry but does not remove it. A stronger test is a corruption that removes the evidence for the answer while keeping the image natural, such as masking only the region the answer comes from, which we did not run.
  • The corruption strength was set on a 30-example smoke test from the model's own answer probability, and only one strength was trained.
  • The second term of the loss (the reference answer must outscore the wrong predicted answer) is our reading of the contrastive term and is the same in both versions. An embedding-level contrastive loss would need a change to the head loss.
  • Mask and shuffle were only used for grading, so they show transfer, not training under those corruptions.
  • Grounding here means the score falls when the image is degraded. It does not show that the score tracks the specific visual evidence for an answer.
  • AUROC differences between old and new loss are inside the seed spread, so this version improves grounding and does not improve ranking.
