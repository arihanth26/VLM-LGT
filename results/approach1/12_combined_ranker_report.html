Approach 1, report 12: one ranker over every correction, and gating it

Ranker v3 chooses among the greedy answer and every correction candidate (crops, contrastive decoding, grounded crops, step-by-step reasoning, sampled reasoning) with features that say which source proposed each candidate. Then a gate decides, per question, whether the correction step runs at all. Qwen3-VL-2B, frozen. Full test splits, 9,517 questions; every choice made on validation (6,420 questions) and test read once. Ranker training: 12,000 training questions.

Builds on report 11 (the learned correction step, ranker v1 and v2). The ranker, its ablation and the sampled-reasoning round are Mehdi's work (branch mehdi/combined-ranker); the independent retraining, the gating and this report were added on feature/gating-escalation.

  • Three sampled reasoning answers per question raise it to +3.60 (+3.10 to +4.09) (86.38%): ChartQA +7.00, ScienceQA +3.32, TextVQA +2.68, DocVQA +1.36. Broken answers fall from 179 to 130.
  • The choice is not one lucky setting. The validation gain (+3.01) matches the test gain, and ranker v3 reaches 85.4–86.0% on ten different candidate sets (Section 5). An independent retraining from the same code on a second copy of the data gives 85.88% against 85.87%.

  • Gating keeps the gain at a fraction of the cost. Correcting only the questions whose greedy answer is least probable (a threshold fixed on validation) gives +3.60 (+3.10 to +4.10) points while correcting 50% of questions, at 7.0 model passes per question instead of 12.0; at 30%, +3.24 (+2.76 to +3.69) at 4.9 passes. Without sampled reasoning, the best setting under 5 passes comes within 0.2 points of the ungated v3 result (85.68% at 3.6 passes, against 85.87% at 24.1).
  • The best gate is the answer's own probability. Across the 30 validation choices of a gate, the answer probability was chosen 28 times and the trained head 2 times (Section 10). The head is the better error detector overall (report 10), but that advantage does not carry over to deciding which questions to correct.

3. What ranker v3 changes

Ranker v2 (report 11) already saw every candidate, but not where a candidate came from. The two correction rounds are good at different things: reasoning fixes ChartQA and ScienceQA, crops help TextVQA and DocVQA. v2 could not exploit this, and adding every source to its candidate set made it worse than reasoning alone. v3 adds two kinds of input and nothing else:

InputPer candidateWhy
Source agreementfor each correction family (zoomed crops, contrastive decoding, grounded crops, reasoning), the share of its proposals equal to this candidate (0 if the family is not offered)lets the ranker learn which family to trust, and that several families agreeing is strong evidence
Datasetone-hot of the question's datasetlets it trust different families on different datasets (Section 6 shows this matters little)

The network is the v2 one: a two-layer MLP with listwise softmax loss, three seeds averaged. Two variants: full_ds reads the answer and question hidden states as well; scalars_ds reads only the scalar features. During training each question gets a random candidate set and a random number of samples, so one ranker serves every configuration. The epoch is chosen on validation; the learning rate and the variant are chosen on validation; the configuration is chosen on validation.

Validation sweep: full_ds at 0.0003: 82.58%, full_ds at 0.001: 82.50%, scalars_ds at 0.0003: 83.20%, scalars_ds at 0.001: 83.55%. The scalar-only variant wins: the hidden states add noise the scalars already summarize.

6. Does the dataset input matter?

The ablation retrains both variants without the dataset one-hot (*_nods), everything else equal. Chosen on validation: greedy + every correction source with ranker_v3_scalars_nods: 85.77%, +2.99 (+2.47 to +3.51) points, against 85.87% with the dataset input. The difference is a tenth of a point: the source-agreement features carry the gain, and the ranker needs no dataset label to use them.

7. Sampled reasoning (round 3)

The single reasoning answer is the strongest correction source, but it is right only about 82% of the time and sometimes never reaches an "Answer:" line. Round 3 samples three more reasoning paths per question (temperature 0.7, one batched call; 384 tokens for ChartQA, 256 otherwise), encodes and judges every new answer like the others, and adds them to the reasoning family, so the ranker sees how many reasoning paths agree. 9,457 of 9,517 test questions got at least one usable sampled answer.

9. Gating: correct only the questions that need it

Without a gate, every question pays for every candidate. The gate keeps the greedy answer when a detector says it is probably right. How the threshold is set: for a target share b of questions to correct, the threshold is the b-quantile of the detector's scores on validation; a test question is corrected when its own score is at or below it. Each decision depends only on that question and on numbers fixed before test, so the share corrected on test is measured, not imposed. The detector and the budget are chosen on validation. Cost is counted per question from what it actually needed: one generation for an uncorrected question (plus the detector's pass if it needs one), the full correction for a corrected one. The rankers are the saved models of Sections 4 and 7, and the ungated rows reproduce their reported accuracy exactly (Section 11).

On validation the answer probability is slightly ahead of the trained head at every budget, so it is the one chosen. On test the head is ahead at 20% (by about 0.4 points) and the two are within 0.1 points at 30% and 50%: they are equivalent within noise, and the answer probability costs no extra pass. The head separates right from wrong greedy answers better overall (report 10: AUROC 0.88 against 0.85), but gating needs something narrower, finding the wrong answers the correction step can actually fix, and there the advantage disappears. The pre-generation score is close behind and decides before the greedy answer exists; the judge is the weakest. Every detector is far above the random gate, which is what makes gating pay.

11. Checks

  • Same inputs. The first-round, second-round and pool files used by ranker v3 are identical (names and sizes) on both copies of the project data; the sampled-reasoning round finished with 0 errors on all 12,000 train, 6,420 validation and 9,517 test questions.
  • Independent retraining. Ranker v3 retrained from the same code and seeds on the second copy: 85.88% on test against 85.87% reported.

12. Limits

  • One model (Qwen3-VL-2B) and four datasets; every number here is in-domain for the ranker.
  • Cost is counted in model passes, not measured time; reasoning calls are longer than short-answer calls (up to 256–384 tokens), so calls that contain reasoning cost more than one short answer.
  • The best possible pick from the candidates is still 92.90%: the ranker captures about 36% of the available gain.
  • DocVQA gains least (about 1.4 points).
